From farmland to urban heat: how robust is the link?

Farmland-to-built-up conversion and land surface temperature in Gia Lam and across Hanoi, 1999 to 2025

Truong Tuan Nghia (2540017)   Nguyen Minh Anh (2540032)   Hoang Ha Dang (2540028)
USTH, Space Applications and Services, group project   26 October 2026

1 Introduction. What? One question, three hypotheses

How is converting farmland into built-up land associated with land surface temperature (LST), and does nearby vegetation or water make a difference?

H1 Mapping

Part of the farmland became built-up land after 2018, and the change can be mapped.

H2 Heat

Converted land warms more than comparable farmland that stayed farmland.

H3 Nature nearby

Converted land closer to vegetation or water is cooler.

We claim an association, not a proven cause. LST is the temperature of the ground surface seen from space, not of the air.

1

1 Introduction. Why? Two-date comparisons hide their own assumptions

What exists

Hanoi studies link built-up land to LST from two dates. Local work documents urbanisation around Gia Lam.

What is missing

Nobody tests the three assumptions: the map is right, the control follows the same path, two clear scenes are comparable.

Why it is hard here

Monsoon cloud leaves few clear hot-season scenes, and the two land-cover products disagree.

Our contribution: a design that tests its own assumptions, over all Landsat scenes 1999 to 2025.

Both groups sit in the same scene, so the weather of the day acts on both.

3 Results. H3 and all of Hanoi (exploratory)

H3, one scene

7 July 2023. Trees: +0.27 °C per log unit of distance (0.16 to 0.38), supports H3. River and ponds: opposite sign.

Hanoi, eight scenes

About 8,000 ha converted. Pooled +0.68 °C (0.36 to 0.95); 13 of 16 districts positive, from -0.73 to +3.40.

Both use the weaker design. The placebo slope (+1.59) is as large as the real one (+1.82).

4 Discussion. A smaller answer, with its limits

Two designs, two answers

Two dates give 1.0 to 2.4 °C depending on the baseline. All 383 scenes give +0.47 °C, with no trend before 2018.

What is solid

Fake breaks give zero; one instrument still positive; sign robust across Hanoi districts.

Data limits

Thermal bands are 60 to 120 m, resampled to 30 m. Landsat 7 has SLC-off gaps and an earlier overpass after 2017. Morning scenes only.

Maps are not yet validated, and post marks a period, not each conversion date: treat 0.5 °C as a research estimate, not a planning figure.

5 Conclusions and perspectives

Conclusions

Converted farmland is about +0.5 °C warmer (0.23 to 0.68; range 0.26 to 0.84), +0.84 °C in the hot season, after 18 years without a pre-trend. Trees help, water does not.

Perspectives

A11 Appendix. Formulas: Level-2 land surface temperature

TK = DN × 0.00341802 + 149.0   ;   LST = TK − 273.15  (°C)
Thermal band ST_B6 (Landsat 5, 7) or ST_B10 (Landsat 8, 9) of the USGS Collection 2 Level-2 product; DN is the stored integer.
Ls = [ Ltoa − Lup − τ (1 − ε) Ldown ] / ( τ ε )
Surface radiance from the at-sensor radiance (TRAD), upwelling (URAD) and downwelling (DRAD) radiance, transmittance τ (ATRAN) and emissivity ε (EMIS).
Ts = K2 / ln( K1 / Ls + 1 )
Landsat 8: K1 = 774.8853, K2 = 1321.0789. Landsat 9: K1 = 799.0284, K2 = 1329.2405. We use the USGS product for the study and this rebuild only for checking.
clear pixel ⇔ QA_PIXEL bits 1, 2, 3 and 4 are all 0
Bits: dilated cloud, cirrus, cloud, cloud shadow. Eight-scene design: footprint ≥ 99 % and clear share ≥ 85 %. The per-pixel uncertainty band ST_QA is in kelvin.

A12 Appendix. Formulas: the Earth Engine recipe we compared against

BT = K2 / ln( K1 / Ltoa + 1 )
Top-of-atmosphere brightness temperature, emissivity taken as 1.
NDVI = ( ρNIR − ρRed ) / ( ρNIR + ρRed )  ,  ρ = DN × 2.75×10−5 − 0.2
Pv = clip[ ( (NDVI − NDVImin) / (NDVImax − NDVImin) )2 , 0 , 1 ]  ;  ε = 0.986 + 0.004 Pv
LSTGEE = BT / [ 1 + ( λ BT / ρc ) ln ε ]
λ = 1.0895×10−5 m; ρc = h c / σ = 1.438×10−2 m K.
No atmospheric term: it reads 6 to 17 °C below Level-2. The atmosphere explains 70 to 90 % of the gap, fixed emissivity another 1.8 to 2.0 °C.

A13 Appendix. Formulas: Sentinel-2 indices and the classifier

NDVI = (B08 − B04) / (B08 + B04)  ;  NDBI = (B11 − B08) / (B11 + B08)  ;  MNDWI = (B03 − B11) / (B03 + B11)
Vegetation, built-up and open-water indices from surface reflectance (SCL mask removes cloud and shadow).
x = [ median(B02, B03, B04, B08, B11, B12), NDVI, NDBI, MNDWI, SDseason(NDVI) ]
10 features per 20 m cell from the May to October median composite of a year. Radar variant adds median and spread of VV and VH (dB).
y ∈ { farmland, built-up, trees, water }   where Esri 2021 = WorldCover 2021
Training pixels are those where two independent products agree. Random forest: 300 trees, minimum 3 samples per leaf, balanced class weights.
accuracy = (1/n) Σ 1[ ŷi = yi ]  ;  κ = ( po − pe ) / ( 1 − pe )
Held out by space: a quarter of the 600 m blocks is never used for training. po is observed and pe chance agreement.

A14 Appendix. Formulas: converted and control groups

sc(p, y) = share of 10 m pixels of class c inside the cell p in year y
Cell = 30 m Landsat pixel for Gia Lam, 120 m cell for Hanoi.
p ∈ C (converted) ⇔ sfarmland(p, 2018) ≥ 0.7  and  sbuilt(p, 2023) ≥ 0.7
Purity threshold TH = 0.7. Esri, our map and the consensus (both maps agree) give three versions of C.
p ∈ K (control) ⇔ sfarmland(p, 2018) ≥ 0.7  and  sfarmland(p, 2023) ≥ 0.7
Stable farmland. A third group, stable built-up land, is used to check sensors and the cold-scene filter.
Aconv = NC × (cell area)
Hanoi: 7,984 ha converted of which 1,048 ha in Gia Lam; Esri 2018 to 2023 at 10 m: 1,517 ha in Gia Lam.
persistence: farmland in 2018 and 2020, built-up in 2023, 2024 and 2025 (own map)

A15 Appendix. Formulas: scene gap and all-scenes regression

gt = LST̄t(C) − LST̄t(K) = Σb SCb,t / Σb NCb,t − Σb SKb,t / Σb NKb,t
S = sum of LST and N = number of clear pixels of group C or K in 600 m block b (20 × 20 cells) in scene t.
gt = α + δ postt + Σj βj Wj,t + γs(t) + θ1 sin(2πdt/365.25) + θ2 cos(2πdt/365.25) + εt
post = 1 from 2020; W = ERA5 air temperature, soil moisture, shortwave radiation, rain over 7 days, dew point (standardised); γ = sensor term; d = day of year. Ordinary least squares.
pre-trend: gt = α + τ yeart + Σ βj Wj,t + γs(t) + θ seasont + εt   (scenes before 2018)
Fake break: replace postt by 1[year ≥ 2008] or 1[year ≥ 2013] on the same scenes; the effect should be 0.
keep scene t ⇔ nC ≥ 400, nK ≥ 1000, nbuilt ≥ 1000 clear pixels  and  LST̄built ≥ Tair − 6 °C
413 scenes pass the first rule, 383 the second (30 removed as too cold from residual cloud or haze).

A16 Appendix. Formulas: uncertainty and dependence

block bootstrap: draw Bb blocks with replacement; recompute all gt and refit the regression; repeat R = 400 times
95 % interval = 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of δ̂(r). Blocks, not pixels, are resampled, and all scenes are recomputed together so covariance between scenes is kept.
ICC ρ = σ2between blocks / ( σ2between blocks + σ2within )
Converted 0.80, control 0.68.
DEFF = 1 + (m − 1) ρ  ;  neff = n / DEFF  ≈  380 of 14,581
m = mean number of pixels per block. Standard error of one scene mean: 0.02 °C if independent, 0.12 by DEFF, 0.24 by block bootstrap (dependence extends across blocks).
δ̂ = ( 1/|Q| ) Σt∈Q gt − ( 1/|P| ) Σt∈P gt  ;  placebo: δ̂ij = gj − gi, i, j ∈ P
Eight-scene estimator and its placebo contrast between two pre-conversion scenes.

A17 Appendix. Formulas: H3, Hanoi dose-response, gap filling

LSTp = α + Σk βk ln(1 + dk,p) + βe elevp + εp ,   k ∈ { trees, Red River, ponds, old core }
H3: converted pixels, 7 July 2023, ordinary least squares with standard errors clustered by 600 m block; d in metres.
ΔLSTb = an(b) + β ΔBuiltb + εb
Blocks of 1.2 km; an = 12 km neighbourhood effect; Δ = 2020 to 2024 scenes minus 2015 to 2018; β is reported per 100 points of built-up share. Placebo: earlier scenes.
RMSE = √ [ (1/m) Σ ( ŷb − yb )2 ]  ;  R2 = 1 − Σ( y − ŷ )2 / Σ( y − ȳ )2
Gap filling: 30 % of 600 m blocks hidden; methods are the district mean, linear interpolation, gradient boosting on same-day Sentinel-2 indices, and boosting plus interpolated residuals.
ŷb = f( NDVI, NDBI, MNDWI, … )b + I( r )b  ,  r = y − f on visible blocks
I = interpolation of the residuals from visible to hidden blocks. Best RMSE 1.9 and 2.1 °C.
