The one research question

How is the conversion of agricultural land into built-up areas associated with changes in land surface temperature in Gia Lam, and can nearby green and blue spaces help explain differences in urban heat?

Method in four steps

Step 1: conversion map
Dynamic World mean probabilities, 2018 and 2025; built-up after and not before; hectares by origin class
Step 2: surface temperature
Landsat LST for the best clear hot-season scene each year; converted vs stable farmland
Step 3: green and blue
NDVI and distance to water around the converted pixels; effect on LST
Step 4: check
About 200 labelled points, three people, kappa; report what is uncertain

Step 1 in detail: where farmland became built-up

  • Dynamic World probabilities averaged in the same months each year
  • Converted = built probability above 0.5 in 2025 and below 0.3 in 2018 (thresholds tested)
  • Origin class from 2018: crops, trees, grass, water, shrub
  • Output: one map and one table of hectares; same map used by steps 2 and 3

Reference size: Gia Lam district gained 654 and 787 ha of urban land per decade (2000-2020).

Step 2 in detail: surface temperature

  • Landsat Level-2: LST = ST_B10 × 0.00341802 + 149.0 − 273.15
  • Mask clouds with the quality band; keep scenes with at least 80% clear pixels in the ROI
  • Window April to September (a published study used 28 April and 25 May)
  • H2: mean LST of converted pixels and of stable farmland, each year, then the difference of the changes

Expected size from the literature: built-up about 6 °C above farmland on one date.

Step 3 in detail: green and blue spaces

  • NDVI from Sentinel-2 or Landsat; water from a water index
  • For each converted pixel: mean NDVI and distance to the nearest water within 800 m
  • Regression of LST on NDVI and distance; water pixels removed from the regression
  • New lakes are few: if fewer than 3, the lake result is a case study, not a test
  • Report the size of the effect with its uncertainty, even if it is small

Labelled points: the simple version

  • 5 classes, 40 points each: stable farmland, converted to built-up, stable built-up, water, vegetation
  • Labelled blind on historical imagery; each member labels one third and 20 shared points
  • Output: confusion matrix, kappa, and a confidence interval for the hectares of H1
  • Points are also reused to check the 2018 and 2025 classes of step 3

Fallback if time runs short: 100 points, state the limit.
