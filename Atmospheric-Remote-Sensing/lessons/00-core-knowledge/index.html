A.1 The radiative transfer equation (lecture notation)

The RTE is the bookkeeping of photons on their way to the sensor.

RTEdI(ν̃)/dl = − kext(ν̃) · I(ν̃) + J(ν̃)
  • I(ν̃) — spectral radiance at wavenumber ν̃ (cm⁻¹) along the path l towards the sensor.
  • kext(ν̃) — extinction coefficient: fraction of the beam removed per unit length.
  • J(ν̃) — source function: everything that adds photons into the line of sight (thermal emission, scattering from other directions).
Extinctionkext = αgas + sgas + αaerosol + saerosol   (absorption + scattering)

α = absorption coefficient, s = scattering coefficient. Gases contribute absorption lines/bands (α) and Rayleigh scattering (s); aerosols contribute both absorption and Mie scattering. This is the same RTE as in chapter 2, written in the notation of the lecture.

A.2 Photon budget along the line of sight

The lecture slide calls it the “radiative transfer budget: estimation of contributions and losses of photons to/from the line of sight”.

ProcessGain / lossRTE termDominant where
Surface reflection into the line of sightGainboundary: (1 − ε_s)·I_sUV–SWIR, bright surfaces
Single scattering into the line of sightGainJ (scattering part)UV–vis: Rayleigh, aerosols
Multiple scattering (≥ 2 events)GainJ (iterated)clouds, thick aerosol, UV
Thermal emission (gas, particle, surface)GainJ = α·B(T); ε_s·B(T_s)thermal IR, microwave
Absorption (atmosphere or surface)Loss− α·Iinside absorption bands
Scattering out of the field of viewLoss− s·Iwherever molecules/particles scatter
Scattering is not destruction
A scattered photon is a loss for one direction and a gain for another. Absorption is the only true loss (energy becomes heat).

A.3 A layer of gas without scattering (thermal infrared)

In local thermodynamic equilibrium and without scattering, the source function is the black-body emission weighted by absorption:

Source functionJ(ν̃) = α(ν̃) · B(ν̃, T)   (emission coefficient = absorption coefficient: Kirchhoff)
Integrated RTEI(ν̃,L) = I(ν̃,0)·e−τ(0,L) + ∫0τ(0,L) B(ν̃, T(l))·e−τ(l,L) dτ
Optical depth from l to the sensorτν̃(l,L) = ∫lL αν̃(l′) dl′
  • Term 1 — background (surface) radiance, attenuated by the whole layer.
  • Term 2 — each slab dτ emits B(T(l)) and is attenuated by the gas between it and the sensor. The factor e−τ(l,L) is what makes the weighting functions of Q3 and Q5.
  • Surface boundary (blackboard): I(ν̃,0) = εs·B(ν̃,Ts) + (1 − εs)·Is, with 0 ≤ εs ≤ 1. The term (1 − εs)·Is is the surface reflection of solar (or downwelling) radiation and 1 − εs = ωs is the surface albedo.
  • Limits: transparent gas (τ ≪ 1) → I ≈ I(0) + τ·B, a small emission added to the background; opaque gas (τ ≫ 1) → I ≈ B(T) of the top layers only: the sensor sees high altitudes (saturation).

A.4 Optical depth and spectroscopy

Continuous spectrum (UV–vis bands)τν̃(L) = Σi ∫0L σi(ν̃, P(l), T(l)) · Ni(l) dl
Line spectrum (IR, microwave)τν̃(L) = Σi Σj ∫0L kiν̃j(T(l)) · Φ(ν̃ − ν̃j, P, T) · Ni(l) dl
Homogeneous layerτi = σi · L · Ni  ;  t = e−τ  ;  τ = α·l = σ·N·l
  • σi — absorption cross-section (cm² molecule⁻¹), known from laboratory spectroscopy (HITRAN).
  • kiν̃j — intensity of line j of species i (depends on T); Φ — line profile, area normalised to 1.
  • Ni — number concentration of the absorber — is our goal (blackboard): σ comes from spectroscopy, L from geometry, so measuring τ gives N.
Example 1 · polluted NO₂ layer (Python check)
  1. L = 1 km = 10⁵ cm; NO₂ ≈ 20 ppb → N = 20×10⁻⁹ × 2.5×10¹⁹ = 5e+11 cm⁻³
  2. τ = 1.5×10⁻¹⁹ × 10⁵ × 5e+11 = 0.0075
  3. t = e−τ = 99.25%
Example 2 · mock exam Q9(c)

σ = 2×10⁻²⁰ cm², N = 5×10¹³ cm⁻³, L = 10⁵ cm ⇒ τ = 0.1, t = e−0.1 ≈ 0.905 (90%).

A.5 Line profiles as a function of altitude

  • Doppler broadening (thermal motion, Gaussian): HWHMD = (ν̃/c)·√(2 ln2·kBT/m). It depends on T only.
  • Lorentz (pressure) broadening (collisions): HWHML = γ₀·(P/P₀)·(T₀/T)ⁿ, γ₀ ≈ 0.07 cm⁻¹ atm⁻¹. Proportional to pressure → ten times narrower every ~16 km.
  • Voigt profile = convolution of both: Lorentz dominates in the troposphere, Doppler above ≈ 20–30 km.
  • Key message (lecture): with known profiles of pressure and temperature, the variation of line widths with altitude gives information on the vertical distribution of the absorbing species. Line centre ↔ high altitudes, line wings ↔ low altitudes.

A.6 DOAS — separating slow and fast spectral structures

Lambert–BeerI(λ) = I0(λ) · exp(−σ(λ) · c · L)
Differential decompositionσ(λ) = σb(λ) + σ′(λ)  ;  D = ln(I′0 / I) = σ′ · c · L
  • High-frequency part (narrow structures) = the gas fingerprint σ′ → fitted with laboratory cross-sections.
  • Low-frequency part = Rayleigh, Mie, surface reflectance and the broad part σb → absorbed by a low-order polynomial.
  • Several gases are fitted at the same time (NO₂, O₃, HCHO, SO₂, BrO…). Output: c·L = slant column; dividing by the AMF gives the vertical column (chapter 3).
Example · mock exam Q10(c)

σ′ = 2×10⁻¹⁹ cm², SCD = 10¹⁶ molecules cm⁻² ⇒ D = 2e-03 = 0.2% of the signal.

Worked example, quick Q&A and memory hooks

Worked example · optical depth of a polluted NO₂ layer
  1. Boundary layer 1 km thick (L = 10⁵ cm), NO₂ ≈ 20 ppb → N = 20×10⁻⁹ × 2.5×10¹⁹ = 5e+11 molecules cm⁻³.
  2. σ′ ≈ 1.5×10⁻¹⁹ cm² (≈ 440 nm) → τ = σ′·L·N = 0.0075.
  3. Transmission e^(−τ) ≈ 99.3%: the NO₂ signature is below 1% of the signal, so DOAS needs very high signal-to-noise and an accurate I₀.

Quick Q&A

Q: What does J contain in the RTE?
A: All photons added into the line of sight: thermal emission α·B and scattering from other directions.
Q: Why is the emission coefficient equal to the absorption coefficient?
A: Kirchhoff’s law, valid in local thermodynamic equilibrium.
Q: For an opaque gas, which layers does the sensor see?
A: Only the top layers: e^(−τ(l,L)) is small for everything deeper.
Q: In τ = σ·N·L, what is unknown?
A: Only N: σ comes from spectroscopy, L from geometry.
Q: Why do line widths tell the altitude?
A: Lorentz width ∝ pressure, and pressure decreases exponentially with height.
Q: Why does DOAS fit a polynomial?
A: To absorb all broadband effects (Rayleigh, Mie, surface) that cannot be separated from σ_b.

Memory hooks

  • The RTE is the photon account book: income (reflection, scattering in, emission) minus spending (absorption, scattering out).RTE = sổ thu chi của photon trên đường tới cảm biến.
  • τ = σ·N·L is walking through a forest: σ = trunk size, N = tree density, L = distance. The denser and longer the forest, the less light gets through.τ = σ·N·L giống đi qua rừng.
  • Low lines are “fat”, high lines “thin”: low down molecules crowd together and collide (like a busy market), so lines blur; high up the air is thin and lines are sharp.Vạch phổ dưới thấp “béo”, trên cao “gầy”.
  • DOAS is hearing a voice over a fan: the steady hum (smooth background) is filtered out, only the fast rhythm of the voice (narrow gas structure) is kept.DOAS như nghe giọng nói giữa tiếng quạt.

Exam checklist — points you must mention

  • RTE in lecture notation: dI/dl = −k_ext·I + J; k_ext = α_gas + s_gas + α_aer + s_aer; list gains and losses.
  • Gas layer without scattering: J = α·B(T); I(L) = I(0)e^(−τ(0,L)) + ∫B(T(l))e^(−τ(l,L))dτ; surface term ε_s B(T_s) + (1 − ε_s) I_s.
  • Optical depth τ = σ·N·L, t = e^(−τ); σ from spectroscopy, N is the unknown.
  • Doppler (∝ √T) vs Lorentz (∝ P) widths; line centre ↔ high altitude, wings ↔ low; DOAS σ = σ_b + σ′ with a polynomial.
Key points (Vietnamese summary from the guide)
  • Phương trình truyền bức xạ: dI/dl = −k_ext·I + J; suy giảm = hấp thụ + tán xạ (của khí và aerosol).
  • Không có tán xạ, cân bằng nhiệt động: J = α·B(T). Bức xạ tới cảm biến = nền bề mặt bị suy giảm + phát xạ của từng lớp bị suy giảm bởi phần khí phía trên.
  • Độ dày quang học τ = σ·N·L: σ lấy từ phổ học, N là mục tiêu cần tìm. Bề mặt: I(0) = ε_s·B(T_s) + (1−ε_s)·I_s, với 1−ε_s là albedo.
  • Độ rộng vạch: Doppler (theo nhiệt độ) và Lorentz (theo áp suất, giảm mạnh theo độ cao) → thông tin về phân bố thẳng đứng. DOAS: tách phần biến đổi nhanh (σ′) khỏi phần biến đổi chậm.
