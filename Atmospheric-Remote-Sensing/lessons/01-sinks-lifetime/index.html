What is a sink?

Picture it: the atmosphere is a bath. Emissions are the tap; sinks are the drains. How long a pollutant stays depends on how fast the drains are.

Technically, a sink is any process that removes a species from the air mass under consideration. The guide distinguishes six types:

SinkMechanismExampleImportant for
Gas-phase oxidationOH radical (also O₃, NO₃ at night, halogens)CO + OH → CO₂ + H; CH₄ + OH → CH₃ + H₂O; NO₂ + OH + M → HNO₃Most reactive gases
PhotolysisUV/visible photons break the moleculeNO₂ + hν (λ < 420 nm) → NO + O; HCHO + hν; CFCs in the stratosphereNO₂, HCHO, CFCs
Dry depositionUptake at the surface (stomata, soil, water), vd; F = −vd·CO₃, SO₂, NH₃, HNO₃, coarse particlesSoluble gases, coarse particles
Wet depositionRainout (in cloud, particles act as CCN) and washout (below cloud)HNO₃, SO₂, NH₃, aerosolsSoluble gases, most aerosols
Heterogeneous chemistryReactions on or inside particles and cloud droplets; gas → particleN₂O₅ → HNO₃; SO₂ → sulfate in waterGas-to-particle conversion
Transport out of the domainExport to the free troposphere or stratosphereN₂O and CFCs go up to the stratosphere and are photolysedLong-lived gases
Do not confuse
Sources (emissions, biology, fires) are not sinks. Gas-to-particle conversion is a sink of the gas and at the same time a source of aerosol.

Lifetime τ and combining sinks

The lifetime tells you "how long, on average, a molecule stays". The formulas of the guide:

Definitionτ = M / L = M / (Fout + Lchem + Ddry + Dwet)  ; steady state: τ = M / P
Parallel sinks1/τ = 1/τchem + 1/τphotolysis + 1/τdry + 1/τwet  ;   τchem = 1/(k·[OH])

Memory aid: rates add, times do not. Two drains open together empty the bath faster than either alone.

Example 1 · CO against OH

k ≈ 2×10⁻¹³ cm³ s⁻¹, [OH] ≈ 1×10⁶ cm⁻³ ⇒ τ = 1/(k[OH]) = 5×10⁶ s ≈ 57.9 days ≈ 2 months. (Python: 57.87 days.)

Example 2 · Three parallel sinks

τ = 2, 3, 6 days ⇒ 1/τ = 0.5 + 0.333 + 0.167 = 1.0 ⇒ τ = 1 day.

Example 3 · Half-life is not the lifetime

For a first-order sink the concentration falls as C/C₀ = e−t/τ. At t = τ, e⁻¹ ≈ 36.8% remains; half is lost after t½ = τ ln 2 ≈ 0.69 τ.

Common mistakes
Confusing τ with the half-life; adding lifetimes instead of their inverses; forgetting to convert seconds to days (divide by 86 400).

Reading lifetime from satellite maps

The core idea is a race between the lifetime τ and the transport time. During τ a molecule travels L ≈ U·τ (U ≈ 5–10 m/s). Mixing around a latitude band takes a few weeks and mixing between the hemispheres about a year.

ClassWhat the satellite map looks likeExamples
Short-lived (hours – days)Map ≈ map of the sources: hotspots at cities, power plants, ships, fires; decay within tens to hundreds of kmNO₂ (TROPOMI), SO₂, HCHO following isoprene
Medium-lived (weeks – months)Plumes and continental-scale transport are visible; main gradient is between hemispheresCO from African/Amazon fires crossing oceans; dust over the Atlantic
Long-lived (years – centuries)Nearly uniform (~1% variability), small north–south gradient and seasonal cycle; very high precision needed to see sourcesCO₂ (OCO-2), CH₄, N₂O

Quantitative rule (Junge relationship): σ/C ≈ 0.14/τ with τ in years — the longer a species lives, the more uniform it is. For CH₄ (τ ≈ 10 years): σ/C ≈ 1.4%.

Deriving τ directly from a plume: downwind of a point source the NO₂ column decays as C(x) ∝ exp(−x/(U·τ)). An exponential fit gives the e-folding length L = U·τ, hence τ = L/U. Example: L = 50 km, U = 5 m/s ⇒ τ = 10⁴ s ≈ 2.8 hours (guide: τNO₂ ≈ 2–6 h).

Exam checklist — points you must mention

  • List ≥ 4 sinks: OH oxidation, photolysis, dry deposition, wet deposition (+ heterogeneous chemistry, transport).
  • Define τ = burden / loss rate; combine sinks with 1/τ = Σ 1/τᵢ.
  • Lifetime vs transport time: short τ → map of sources; long τ → well mixed.
  • Give orders of magnitude: hours (NO₂), days (SO₂), week (aerosols), months (CO), decade (CH₄), century (N₂O, CO₂).
Key points (Vietnamese summary from the guide)
  • Bể chứa: oxy hóa bởi OH, quang phân, lắng đọng khô, lắng đọng ướt, phản ứng dị thể, vận chuyển ra khỏi vùng.
  • Thời gian sống τ = lượng tồn trữ / tốc độ mất; nhiều quá trình song song: 1/τ = Σ 1/τᵢ.
  • So τ với thời gian vận chuyển: τ ngắn → bản đồ trùng nguồn (NO₂ đô thị); τ dài → đồng đều (CO₂, CH₄).
  • Bậc độ lớn: giờ (NO₂), ngày (SO₂), tuần (aerosol, O₃), tháng (CO), chục năm (CH₄), thế kỷ (N₂O, CO₂).
