M2 · Light–matter interaction
Interaction between light and matter · Study guide chapter 2
Exam question 2 (original wording)
  • Describe the main physical mechanisms of interaction between light and matter that modify the intensity of radiation reaching a radiometric sensor.
  • Provide a clear description, the main equations, and schematics of each of these mechanisms.
Contents: The three mechanisms, in plain words · Absorption · Scattering · Emission · Beer–Lambert and optical depth · The radiative transfer equation (RTE) · Exam checklist · Slides · Quiz

The three mechanisms, in plain words

Light travelling from its source (the Sun or the ground) to the sensor is changed in three ways. Absorption is like tinted glass swallowing some light. Scattering is like fog bending rays into other directions. Emission is the warm atmosphere itself glowing in the infrared.

Absorption and scattering both remove energy from the beam (together called extinction); emission and scattering from other directions add energy. Surface reflection also matters for the radiance seen from space.

Model answer (English)

Radiation travelling from the source (Sun or Earth surface) to the sensor is modified by three processes: absorption and scattering (which together form extinction) remove energy from the beam, while emission and scattering into the beam add energy. Surface reflection also matters for the radiance seen from space.

Absorption

A molecule absorbs a photon when its energy matches a transition: E = hν = E₂ − E₁. Every gas has its own absorption spectrum σ(λ) — the “fingerprint” used to identify it.

Transition typeSpectral regionExamples
ElectronicUV – visibleO₃ Hartley–Huggins 250–350 nm; NO₂ 400–450 nm; SO₂ 300–320 nm
Vibrational–rotationalInfraredCO₂ 15 µm and 4.3 µm; O₃ 9.6 µm; CH₄ 7.7 and 2.3 µm; CO 4.6 and 2.3 µm; H₂O
Pure rotationalMicrowave / far-IRH₂O, O₂ 60 GHz, ClO
Absorption coefficientka(λ) = σa(λ) · n   (n: number density, molecule cm⁻³)

The line shape depends on pressure (Lorentz broadening, dominant in the lower atmosphere) and temperature (Doppler broadening, dominant at high altitude).

Scattering

A photon is redirected but keeps its wavelength (elastic). The angular distribution is the phase function P(Θ). The regime depends on the size parameter x = 2πr/λ (see module 7).

TypeParticlesFeatures
Rayleigh (x ≪ 1)Molecules N₂, O₂σ ∝ λ⁻⁴; symmetric P(Θ) = ¾(1 + cos²Θ); blue sky
Mie (x ~ 1)Aerosols, cloud dropletsWeak λ dependence (τ ∝ λ^−α); strong forward peak
Raman (inelastic)N₂, O₂ (rotational)Changes wavelength; causes the Ring effect (fills in Fraunhofer lines) in DOAS
Single-scattering albedoω0 = σs / (σs + σa)   (= 1 for non-absorbing particles; ≈ 0.85 for smoke)
Example · Rayleigh

Scattering at 450 nm compared with 650 nm = (650/450)⁴ = 4.35 times, which is why blue light is scattered more than red.

Emission

Any body with T > 0 K emits. A black body follows the Planck function; real media emit ε·B. In LTE, Kirchhoff’s law holds: ελ = αλ — a gas that absorbs at a wavelength also emits at that wavelength.

PlanckBλ(T) = (2hc² / λ⁵) · 1 / (exp(hc / λkBT) − 1)
Wien and Stefan–Boltzmannλmax · T = 2898 µm·K  ;  M = σSB T⁴
Example · numbers checked in Python
  • Sun (5778 K): λmax = 0.50 µm (visible).
  • Earth (288 K): λmax = 10.1 µm (thermal IR); M = 390 W m⁻².

Emission dominates for λ > ~4 µm (thermal IR, microwave), while reflected sunlight dominates in the UV – visible – near-IR.

Beer–Lambert and optical depth

ExtinctiondIλ = −ke(λ) Iλ ds  ;  ke = ka + ks = Σ σi ni
SolutionIλ(s) = Iλ(0) · e−τλ  ;  τλ = ∫ ke(λ,s) ds  ;  T = e−τ

Slant path: τslant = τvertical / cos θ (plane-parallel atmosphere).

The radiative transfer equation (RTE)

RTEdIλ/ds = −ke Iλ + ka Bλ(T) + (ks/4π) ∫ P(Θ) Iλ(Ω′) dΩ′
  • Term 1 = extinction (loss).
  • Term 2 = thermal emission (gain).
  • Term 3 = light from other directions scattered into the line of sight (gain).

Two simplified cases in common use

Thermal IR without scattering (Schwarzschild)Iλ(TOA) = εs Bλ(Ts) Tλ(0,∞) + ∫ Bλ(T(z)) ∂Tλ(z,∞)/∂z dz
UV – visible (DOAS)ln(I0/I) = Σ σi′ · SCDi + smooth polynomial

Lambertian surface: I = A·F0·cos θ0 / π (A: albedo).

Exam checklist — points you must mention

  • Three mechanisms: absorption (quantised transitions, σ(λ) fingerprint), scattering (Rayleigh/Mie, phase function, ω₀), emission (Planck, Kirchhoff).
  • Beer–Lambert: I = I₀ e^−τ, τ = ∫ k_e ds, k_e = k_a + k_s.
  • Write the full RTE and identify each term; Schwarzschild for thermal IR.
  • Draw: absorbing slab, scattering particle with arrows in all directions, emitting layer, exponential decay curve.
Key points (Vietnamese summary from the guide)
  • Ba cơ chế: hấp thụ (chuyển mức năng lượng điện tử, dao động, quay → mỗi khí có dấu vân tay phổ), tán xạ (đổi hướng photon: Rayleigh ∝ λ⁻⁴, Mie), phát xạ nhiệt (Planck, Kirchhoff ε = α).
  • Hấp thụ + tán xạ = suy giảm (extinction), tuân theo định luật Beer–Lambert I = I₀·e^(−τ).
  • Phương trình truyền bức xạ: suy giảm − phát xạ + tán xạ vào hướng nhìn.
