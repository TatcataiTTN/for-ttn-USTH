Three mechanisms change the radiation

Absorption
A photon is absorbed when E = hν = E₂ − E₁.
Each gas has a fingerprint σ(λ).
Scattering
Direction changes, wavelength does not.
Rayleigh (molecules), Mie (particles).
Emission
A body at temperature T emits ε·Bλ(T).
Dominant for λ > ~4 µm.

Absorption + scattering = extinction; emission and scattering into the line of sight add radiation.

Scattering: Rayleigh, Mie and Raman

  • Rayleigh (molecules, x ≪ 1): σ ∝ λ−4 → blue sky
  • Mie (aerosols, x ~ 1): weak λ dependence, forward peak
  • Raman: inelastic → Ring effect
Rayleigh phase functionP(Θ) = ¾ (1 + cos²Θ)
Single-scattering albedoω0 = σs / (σs + σa)

ω₀ = 1: no absorption; ω₀ ≈ 0.85: smoke (about 15% absorption).

Phase functions from Mie theory

The larger the particle, the more the scattering is concentrated forward (asymmetry parameter g ≈ 0.6–0.8 for aerosols).

The three mechanisms in one figure

  • Absorption and scattering remove energy from the beam
  • Emission and scattering from other directions add energy to the beam

The radiative transfer equation (RTE)

dIλ/ds = −ke Iλ + ka Bλ(T) + (ks/4π) ∫ P(Θ) Iλ(Ω′) dΩ′
Term 1: extinction
Energy lost by absorption and by scattering out of the beam
Term 2: emission
The medium emits thermal radiation following Planck
Term 3: scattering in
Light from all other directions scattered into the line of sight

Key points

  • Absorption + scattering = extinction (Beer–Lambert I = I₀e−τ)
  • Emission follows Planck; Kirchhoff ε = α
  • RTE: extinction − emission + scattering into the line of sight
  • Rayleigh ∝ λ−4 gives the blue sky
