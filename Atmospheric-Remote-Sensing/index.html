Suggested study path

  1. Start here: read the 12 cards (15 minutes) and pass the 12-question check (10/12).
  2. Part A and Part B, then the eight chapters: read the plain-language part, the formulas, the worked example, Q&A and memory hooks; look at the slides and the lecture pages.
  3. Parts C and D: the lecture’s satellite case studies and lidar (new material, with the lecture exercises solved).
  4. Practise the numbers in the calculators, then take the mock papers: Paper 5 (drill), Papers 2–4, finally Paper 1 (official) under time.

Status and limits (honest notes)

  • Sources: (1) the study pack v2 supplied by the student (guide, mock exam, 24 figures, 11 class photos); (2) the lecture master “Remote Sensing of the Atmosphere: a focus on air composition” (J. Cuesta, UPEC; 233 pages), read by text layer plus OCR; 137 selected pages are shown with credit and every number taken from them is cited by slide number. No textbook was supplied, so there are no book page numbers.
  • Numbers: every calculated value is recomputed in Python (numpy/scipy) with two independent methods; the JavaScript calculators are compared with Python in 478 checks. The simple models (TIR brightness-temperature depression, O₂ above the layer, the 12-level averaging-kernel simulation, the line-width model) are illustrations, not measured data.
  • Big 12 coverage: the “95% skeleton / 61% complete” figures are my own A/B/C assessment of 122 parts of the five papers; Papers 2–5 were written by me in the style of the official paper. The real exam is unknown.
  • Questions: “Original” questions come from the mock exam, the checklists, the guide and the lecture slides (each has its source); “Added” questions are extra items. Bias audit over all 260 multiple-choice questions: the correct answer is at position A/B/C/D in 27/26/24/23% of the questions, and is the longest option in 31% and the shortest in 32% (unbiased would be 25% each). Length is only roughly balanced.
  • Language: English by default; each lesson keeps the short Vietnamese summary. No Chinese version.
  • Figures: 24 from the original pack, 7 new (hydrostatic law, sinks, ozone cycle, multispectral ozone, lidar exercise, DIAL, Big 12 map); wide figures are scaled on slides, so text inside a figure can be smaller than 20 px (all HTML slide text is ≥ 20 px).
  • Discrepancies found: guide v2 Ch.8 quotes brightness-temperature depressions of ≈ 7 K (4 km) and ≈ 2 K (1 km); our simple model gives larger values (same trend). Lecture exercise 1(d): the printed solution is 8.6 nW; recomputation with exp(−2τ) gives 9.0 nW (same conclusion). The lecture slide says pressure halves every “≈ 5 km”; H ln 2 = 5.75 km.
  • Copyright: the lecture pages belong to their author and are shown for private study with credit; the full lecture PDF is not republished.
