## Introduction

**s5 [seen]** Title: "The radiative transfer equation provides the theoretical frame to understand light and matter interaction in the water body."
Equation as printed: dL(θ,φ)/dr = −c·L(θ,φ) + 2π ∫₀^{4π} β(θ,φ,θ″,φ′) L dω′ + S. Sketch: a thin slab of thickness dr; a thick black arrow L(θ,φ) enters and leaves slightly weaker; a second arrow L(θ′,φ′) is scattered into the beam ("Gain by scattering (b)"); an arrow leaving sideways is "Loss by scattering (b)"; "Loss by absorption (a)". Brace on the right: **b + a = c**.
Note (ours): the "2π" prefactor in front of the integral and the 0–4π limits are as printed; the textbook writes the in-scattering term as ∫ β(ψ) L dω′ over 4π without the 2π. We keep the slide form for the course and flag the difference (see textbook/formulas.md).

## IOPs (s11–19)

**s14 [seen]** "The basic concept of Optical Case 1 and Case 2 waters of Morel and Prieur (1977)". Source credit: IOCCG, 2000. Left: a ternary triangle with corners P (phytoplankton, top), Y (yellow substances = CDOM, bottom left), S (suspended material, bottom right); the top band is "Case 1", the rest "Case 2". Right: the same triangle with 20 % grid lines and a point "x" read off along each axis (how to read a ternary diagram).
Message: Case 1 = optical properties covary with phytoplankton (P corner); Case 2 = Y and S vary independently of P.

**s16 [seen]** "The Optical Water Class frame work is more suitable to account for the optical variability and complexity of natural waters." Credit: Loisel et al., 2023. Global map (three-lobe projection) of water classes 1–17; colour bar: 1 black/red, 2 brown, 3–8 green→yellow-green, 9–17 blue→violet/magenta; white at the end.
Reading: violet/magenta (classes ~14–17) fills the subtropical gyres (clearest water); blue (9–13) the mid-latitude open ocean; green/yellow-green (3–8) coastal zones and upwellings; red (1–2) polar/high-latitude and the most turbid shelf areas. Class colours are only qualitative; the numeric meaning of each class (Chl range, etc.) is NOT on the slide — not verified.

**s17 [seen]** Header "I – The inherent optical properties (IOPs)". "The two main IOPs are: the absorption a (m⁻¹) and the volume scattering function (angular distribution of the scattered power) β (m⁻¹ sr⁻¹)."
a(λ) ≡ lim_{Δr→0} (1/Φ_i(λ))·(Φ_a(λ)/Δr) [m⁻¹]; β(ψ,λ) ≡ lim_{Δr→0} lim_{ΔΩ→0} Φ_s(ψ,λ)/(Φ_i(λ) Δr ΔΩ) [m⁻¹ sr⁻¹]. Figure: a small volume ΔV of thickness Δr; incident flux Φ_i from the left; absorbed Φ_a inside; transmitted Φ_t; scattered Φ_s(ψ) into solid angle ΔΩ at angle ψ.

**s18 [seen]** "The other IOPs can be derived from a and β." b = ∫_{4π} β(θ)dω; with revolution symmetry b = 2π∫₀^π β(θ) sinθ dθ; forward b_f = 2π∫₀^{π/2}…; backward b_b = 2π∫_{π/2}^{π}…; b = b_f + b_b; **c = a + b**.

**s19 [seen]** Additivity: a(λ) = a_w + a_CDOM + a_ph + a_NAP; b(λ) = b_w + b_p; b_b(λ) = b_bw + b_bp. "This additivity allows the development of separate models for the IOPs of the various constituents of sea water."

## Pure water (s20–27)

**s20 [seen]** "Volume scattering function of pure sea water". β_w(θ,λ,S,T=20 °C) = 1.38 (λ/500 nm)^−4.32 (1 + 0.3 S/37) 10⁻⁴ (1 + cos²2θ (1−δ)/(1+δ)) — Morel, 1974. Boxes: the 1.38/−4.32 part is a "fit using theoretical calculations"; the (1+0.3S/37) part is "fixed for lab experiments". S = salinity, T = temperature, δ = depolarisation factor (0.09). "Calculated from the Einstein–Smoluchowski theory (scattering is due to the fluctuation of density of the medium, accentuated by fluctuation of the ionic concentration)". "S impact: 30 %".
Note (ours): the printed "cos2θ" is read as cos²θ, the standard angular term (1+cos²θ·(1−δ)/(1+δ)) — see formulas_from_slides.md; units of β_w are m⁻¹ sr⁻¹ (the figure on s22 shows 10⁻⁴ scale).

**s22 [seen]** Axis: β_w(ψ,λ)·10⁴ [m⁻¹ sr⁻¹] on a LOG scale (10⁻¹…10²) versus ψ = 0–180°. Three nearly parallel U-shaped curves labelled 350, 450, 550 nm: highest at ψ = 0 and 180°, minimum at 90°; shorter λ = higher curve. Caption (French, translated): "Figure 6 – Scattering phase function of pure sea water for different wavelengths (in Loisel, 1999)". Read from graph (~10 %): at 90° β_w·10⁴ ≈ 6.5 (350 nm), ≈ 2.2 (450 nm), ≈ 0.95 (550 nm); at 0°/180° ≈ 12, 4, 1.7. Our Python check is in verified.json.

**s23 [seen]** "20 years later": β_w = **1.29** (λ/500 nm)^**−4.14** (1+0.3S/37) 10⁻⁴ (1 + cos2θ(1−δ)/(1+δ)) [printed exactly as "cos2θ" at 250 dpi, no superscript; standard Morel form is cos²θ — to be checked with textbook/Python], δ = **0.051** — Buiteveld et al. (1994). Box: "The spectral dependency in −4.32 becomes −4.14 due to the reformulation of the refractive index, and to the fact that it now accounts for the compressibility isotherm of water." Question on slide: "How much for air molecules?" (answer = Rayleigh, λ⁻⁴; not on slide).

**s24 [seen]** "Absorption and scattering spectra of pure sea water". Log y-axis (m⁻¹) 10⁻³…10⁰, λ = 400–700 nm. a_w rises from ≈ 0.005 at ~415 nm (minimum) to ≈ 0.6 at 700 nm, with steps/shoulders near 520–530, 575–600 and ~610 nm. b_w falls smoothly from ≈ 0.007 at 400 nm to ≈ 0.0007 at 700 nm (a straight line on a log plot). The curves cross near 430–440 nm (a_w = b_w ≈ 0.005). Caption: a_w from Pope & Fry (1997), b_w from Morel (1974).

## Phytoplankton absorption (s28–34)

**s29 [seen]** Bricaud et al. (2004), Fig. 1: weight-specific absorption spectra a*_sol,i(λ) (m² mg⁻¹) of pigments, 400–700 nm. Photosynthetic carotenoids (red) and non-photosynthetic (blue) shown. Chl a: peaks ≈ 440 and ≈ 675 nm (peak ≈ 0.033 at 675). Chl c₁,c₂ (light blue): very high narrow peak ≈ 0.076 at ≈ 460 nm and small bumps at ≈ 585/635 nm. Chl b: ~470 & ~650 nm. Carotenoids (fucoxanthin, peridinin, α/β-carotene, zeaxanthin, alloxanthin, diadinoxanthin, 19′-BF, 19′-HF): broad absorption 450–520 nm, ≈ 0 above ~550 nm. ⇒ Everything absorbs in blue, only Chl (and Chl b/c) absorb in the red near 675/650/635 nm.

**s32 [seen]** "Variability of a*_phy (a factor of 4): discretisation (package) effect or/and internal pigment concentration?" Top-left: Bricaud et al. (2004), a_φ(440) (m⁻¹) vs [Tchl a] (mg m⁻³), log–log, 0.01–30; points follow a straight line (solid = "this study", dashed = Bricaud 1995) – "HPLC only" box. Bottom: Bricaud et al. (1995), a*_ph(412) and a*_ph(443) vs <Chl> (0.01–100 mg m⁻³): a*_ph is high (0.1–0.15 m² mg⁻¹) at low Chl and falls to ≈ 0.01–0.02 at Chl ≈ 10–20. i.e. specific absorption decreases as Chl increases.

**s33 [seen]** "Discretisation effect". Equations (Bricaud 2004): Q*_a(λ) = a_φ(λ)/a_sol(λ) (3) — "decreases from 1 (no package effect) to 0 (maximal package effect)"; a_sol(λ) = Σ C_i a*_sol,i(λ) (4) (pigments in solvent shifted to in-vivo positions). Right top: Q*_a(440) vs [Tchl a] — decreases from ≈ 1 at 0.03–0.1 to ≈ 0.4 at 10–30 mg m⁻³. Right bottom: "size index" (µm) vs [Tchl a]: 1–3 µm (100 % picoplankton) at low Chl up to ≈ 10–30+ (100 % microplankton) at high Chl. ⇒ package effect increases with Chl because cells are larger.

**s34 [seen]** a_pigm(λ) = Σ C_i a*_sol,i(λ). Left: a*_pigm(440) (m² mg⁻¹) vs [Tchl a]: scatter between ≈ 0.05 and 0.09 (arrow marks a*_sol,chl a ≈ 0.03 level at bottom) — pigment composition effect is modest compared with package effect. Right: "Photoadaptation": four spectra of a*_φ(λ) (m² mg⁻¹, 350–750 nm); the "High light" curve is the lowest (≈ 0.014 at 440 nm vs ≈ 0.035 for the top curve) — acclimated to high light has lower specific absorption; red peak ≈ 675 nm. Equation a*_φ(λ) = a_φ(λ)/Chl.

## CDOM and a_cdm (s39–45)

## IOPs ↔ biogeochemistry (s63–84)

**s63 [seen]** "II – Link between IOPs and 'biogeochemical' parameters". IOP(λ) = Σ_i (N_i/V)·σ_IOP,i(λ) = Σ_i (N_i/V)·Q_IOP,i(λ)·S_i. Boxes: σ = IOP cross-section (m²); N/V = number of particles in m⁻³; Q = IOP efficiency factor (dimensionless); S = geometrical area (m²). "Q: the ratio of the radiant flux absorbed or scattered by a particle to the radiant flux incident onto the geometrical cross-section of this particle".

**s64 [seen]** Sketch of a polygonal particle with three coloured rays: red (refraction), green (reflection), blue (diffraction). Text: "Large particles: scattering is dominated by diffraction, since light going through the particles is likely to be absorbed → geometric optics; response is proportional to particle's cross-sectional area (sensitive to shape). Small particles: scattering is dominated by refraction and reflection, Rayleigh scattering; response is proportional to particle's volume (insensitive to shape). In between (for a sphere): Mie scattering. Analytical solutions exist for spheroids as well."
