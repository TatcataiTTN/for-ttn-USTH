Sources, checks and limits

Sources

  • Lecture: Marine Optics, Hubert Loisel, Laboratoire d’Océanologie et de Géosciences (LOG), file “Optique-marine-English-UBATUBA”, 120 slides. Slide figures shown here are small selected pages with credit to the lecturer; each slide also credits its original authors (Bricaud, Babin, Snyder, Boss, Claustre…).
  • Textbook: Ocean Optics Web Book, footer: “2020 Creative Commons Attribution license” (CC BY 2.0). We cite page titles and URLs and do not copy its images; all curves on this site are our own computations.
  • Home-made checks: build/verify_numbers.py → data/verified.json: 91 cross-checks, 91.0 passed, discrepancies: 0.

What was verified, and how

  • Pure-water bw: numerical integration vs closed form; exponent ratios vs numerical integral of the whole VSF.
  • Mie computations (Bohren–Huffman series with spherical Bessel functions): Qsca by series vs angular quadrature; Rayleigh limit vs closed form; Qext vs anomalous diffraction; Junge ensemble exponents by two fitting methods.
  • Exponential laws: log-fit vs nonlinear fit; Beer–Lambert: closed form vs ODE; package effect: closed form vs ray-path quadrature; euphotic/Secchi: closed form vs root finding.
  • Browser calculators are tested in Node against Python (tests/test_calc.js).

Where the lecture and the textbook disagree (kept, not hidden)

  • Radiative transfer equation: slide 5 prints a factor 2π before the scattering integral; the textbook writes ∫β L dω′ over 4π without it.
  • Pure-water VSF: the slide formula at 546 nm, S = 0 gives β(90°) = 0.944×10⁻⁴ m⁻¹ sr⁻¹ (Morel form) and 0.896×10⁻⁴ (Buiteveld form); the textbook quotes 0.869×10⁻⁴ (its own computation from listed constants gives 0.854×10⁻⁴, 1.7 % lower) and recalls 0.83×10⁻⁴ for Morel (1974). We did not find the convention that reconciles them.
  • Angular term: the slide prints “cos2θ”; we read cos²θ because it reproduces bw(500) ≈ 3×10⁻³ m⁻¹ read from slide 24 (2.88×10⁻³), while cos(2θ) would give 1.63×10⁻³. Evidence, not proof.
  • Depolarisation: 0.09 (Morel) and 0.051 (Buiteveld) on the slides vs ρ = 0.031 (Zhang et al. 2019) in the textbook.
  • Junge slope: the lecture uses N(D) ∝ D−ξ with ξ = γ + 3; the textbook’s Junge law is dN/dlogD ∝ D−3 ⇔ n(D) ∝ D−4, so the same distribution has ξ = 4 in the lecture notation.

Honest limits

  • No sample exam, no syllabus. The lecturer’s exam is unknown. “Coverage” numbers describe only the five papers I wrote; they are not a prediction.
  • All questions are mine. No original questions existed, so every item is labelled “bo_sung” (added) and carries its slide or textbook source.
  • No AOP slides in the lecture file. Outline slide 9 lists “Link between IOPs and the light field”, but module G is textbook-only and labelled so. The relation R ≈ f bb/(a+bb) was not found on the crawled textbook pages and is only used qualitatively (classical result, Gordon et al. 1975).
  • Values read from graphs are approximate (≈ 10–20 %) and always labelled; some slides (s4, 6, 7 images, 73, 78 and the small web-viewer screenshots 116–118) were not read in detail and are used only through their text.
  • Mie spheres are a model; they are used to check exponents and trends, not to claim measured values. Simple models are illustrations, not data.
  • Progress is stored in your browser (localStorage). No Chinese version. Slide images remain the property of their authors.
